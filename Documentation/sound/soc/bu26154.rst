==================================
ROHM BU26154 audio support status
==================================

Supported configuration
=======================

``sound/soc/codecs/bu26154.c`` provides I2C ASoC codec support for one
capture path and one speaker playback path: 44.1 kHz, S16_LE, two I2S slots,
with the codec as clock consumer and an external 512fs MCLK. The mono ADC
signal occupies the left slot. Capture and playback are mutually exclusive.
Both playback slots feed
the mono speaker; headphone output and other PCM formats are not advertised.

``Speaker Playback Switch`` enables the DAPM output route and defaults off.
``Playback Volume`` defaults to mute: value 0 is mute, and values 1--143
represent -71.5 to -0.5 dB in 0.5 dB steps. The prohibited PDATT value 0xff
is not exposed. Analog attenuation is fixed at -12 dB, speaker boost is
disabled, and headphone amplifiers and their supply remain off. Playback
power sequencing has software coverage; a basic user-reported integration
check is recorded below. Electrical sequencing and rail switching remain
unvalidated.

The driver does not register a sound card or enable a board description.
Its MCLK provider must deliver nominal 22.5792 MHz when enabled during ASoC
component probe. The driver accepts up to 0.1% divider rounding; a
22,573,242 Hz clock falls within that tolerance. This is a driver policy,
not a datasheet accuracy specification.

Codec configuration
===================

The binding describes the I2C device, its MCLK and four input supplies.
HPVDD, HPVSS, REGOUT and VMID are generated outputs. The chip has no
documented silicon ID; enumeration identifies it, while a MAPCON read checks
that accesses are in register map 0. A nonzero bank is rejected without
changing the bank or resetting the device.

The register settings follow BU26154MUV Rev.002:

* MIC MINVOL is physically in bits 7:5; MINDIF is bit 1. The volume control
  uses shift 5 and a 6--27 dB range in 3 dB steps. Bits 4:2 and 0 are
  reserved, so treating MINVOL[2:0] as register bits 2:0 is incorrect.
* SR=7 selects 44.1 kHz; CLKSEL=6 selects external 512fs MCLK without the
  PLL. CLKEN enables MCLK input and PLLOE, including in PLL-bypass mode.
* SAITX and SAIRX are both set to 0x80 for 16-bit I2S. SAIMODE at read
  address 0x64 controls clock-provider mode and byte swapping; zero selects
  clock-consumer mode without byte swapping.
* Rate and serial format are changed only while RECPLAY is stopped. Capture
  uses VMID fast-charge and normal modes, with a conservative 100 ms fast
  charge followed by at least 5 ms normal-mode settling. Playback follows
  the same reference settling before enabling its DAC and speaker path.
  MCTIME=32 specifies 4096/fs, about 92.9 ms at 44.1 kHz, including DSP
  initialization; both paths wait 100 ms after their RECPLAY command.

The register map uses even read and odd write indexes. Regmap-I2C applies
``write_flag_mask = BIT(0)`` and ``reg_stride = 2``; raw I2C is required
because the SMBus register-operation fallback does not apply the write flag.
Single-register transfers and a register whitelist avoid assumptions about
sequential access or cached state. Regmap paging selects map 1 for speaker
input configuration, preserving the even-read/odd-write index convention.

DAPM controls the shared audio clock and each stream's power sequence.
Stream startup holds a standard CCF reference through ``hw_params``, mute
and ``hw_free`` register access, releasing it at shutdown. DAPM holds an
independent reference while the power path is active; the component's
initialization reference is temporary. No card-specific clock lease is
required for codec register access.

Power-up failures are recorded because this kernel's DAPM event errors do
not fail PCM prepare; the subsequent PCM START is rejected. Power-down
attempts continue after an I2C
error. A failed component probe performs its own best-effort unwind.

MXS clock and card integration
==============================

The MXS SAIF MCLK provider uses the generic divider helpers with hardware
values 0--4 representing divisors 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16. Reserved values are
excluded and ``CLK_GET_RATE_NOCACHE`` rereads hardware. CCF enable/disable
callbacks coordinate reset, RUN and CLKGATE with legacy and PCM ownership;
they do not call the legacy API or acquire its mutex. The output provider
does not infer or propagate a functional-parent rate from MCLK alone.

``mxs_saif_get_mclk()`` and ``mxs_saif_put_mclk()`` hold one legacy physical
MCLK lease: a second get returns ``-EBUSY``. PCM generator users are counted
separately, so stopping one cannot stop another's clocks. A retiring PCM
retains ownership through DMA synchronization and physical settling.
Each DAI obtains a distinct consumer handle through the master's OF clock
provider, with managed cleanup on the consuming device. A slave must not use
the provider-only managed clock helper against its master's clock hardware.
Each configured DAI also holds a CCF rate-exclusive reference until
``hw_free`` completes that drain and settling. Matching configurations may
share the generator, while a differing rate, MCLK or frame width is refused.
Paused streams retain protection. Failed clock transactions restore their
owned divider/base fields and parent rate on a best-effort basis without
overwriting unrelated fields or another consumer's exclusive ownership.

The existing ``fsl,saif-master`` phandle establishes a managed device link.
A slave defers until its master is fully bound, then keeps a per-device
pointer valid through component teardown. Runtime stream callbacks do not
look up their master in the legacy global registry. Legacy card users must
also retain the provider for their MCLK lease and balance it after card
teardown.

SAIF_CTRL updates share a register lock with the divider provider. Masked
updates preserve unrelated RUN, format and interrupt fields across clock
calls; register locks are not held across CCF calls.

``simple-audio-card`` can use ordinary directional links: SAIF0 for playback
and SAIF1 for capture. A capture-only card does not need a dummy playback
link: master initialization and capture configuration establish SAIF0's
framing independently of a playback DAI link. No codec-specific machine
driver is needed. The mandatory MCLK handle supplies the actual rate; the
codec does not require a ``set_sysclk()`` callback.

Both SAIF instances advertise the same DAI type name. Simple-card therefore
adds a link index to runtime names on multi-link cards, keeping their sysfs
devices distinct. Single-link names remain unchanged. Directional links are
still ordinary playback/capture PCMs, not a full-duplex codec guarantee.

For a fixed-rate link, the CPU endpoint needs nominal 22.5792 MHz sysclk,
not SAIF1's internal-logic clock rate. The master functional rate and divider
must be configured before component initialization. A link ``mclk-fs`` setting
programmed in PCM hw_params is too late to supply the initialization clock.

At the CLKCTRL layer, the fractional divider and its hardware gate must be
one rate-and-gate composite with ``CLK_SET_RATE_UNGATE``. This gives the rate
transaction a counted enable reference before programming DIV and through
BUSY polling. The public SAIF clock names and IDs remain unchanged, with
1:1 leaf clocks rather than duplicate owners of the gate bit. The gate must
already be open before DIV changes (i.MX28 Reference Manual Rev.2,
HW_CLKCTRL_SAIF0, p.922); it cannot be opened in the same DIV write. This is
independent of the physical MCLK ownership implemented at SAIF_CTRL.
Stuck-BUSY recovery remains outside the validated scope.

``audio-graph-card`` uses the same clock helpers and offers no additional
clock-management advantage for this link. Assigned functional-parent and
output rates establish the initial frequency; a counted CCF enable supplies
the physical clock. A successful rate readback alone cannot prove pin activity
or clock-domain transfer. Further physical generic-card lifecycle validation
remains required.

Keep board DTS and configuration disabled until clock ownership and actual
supply wiring are established. Broader playback validation, 24-bit support, system
suspend/resume, live master/slave unbind and clock-fault recovery remain
outside the validated scope.

Validation
==========

The codec object built for ARM with ``W=1`` without warnings. The binding and
compiled example passed ``dt-doc-validate``, ``dt-mk-schema`` and
``dt-validate``. Strict ``checkpatch.pl --file`` reported no codec-source
issues. These checks establish build and schema validity, not electrical
operation.

Eight additional ARM-QEMU software cases cover sequential capture/playback,
power failure unwind, mute-error latching, clock and stream constraints,
component teardown, real regmap paging with odd write indexes, and stream
clock lifetime using actual ASoC startup/shutdown wrappers. The
fixture substitutes register transport and clocks; it does not exercise a
physical speaker or the complete card lifecycle.

The ``mxs-saif-clk`` KUnit cases, enabled by
``CONFIG_SND_SOC_MXS_SAIF_KUNIT_TEST``, cover supported ratios, the default
without external MCLK, invalid requests, overflow and one-Hz readback rounding.
An additional ARM-QEMU run passed 31 clock-helper and actual-driver/CCF
lifecycle cases, including configured-rate ownership, capture-only framing,
ASoC START failure wrappers, transaction rollback and actual OF consumer
lookup/managed cleanup. Registers and DMA
boundaries were simulated; this is not complete ALSA card or hardware testing.

A separate ARM-QEMU fixture exercises the actual simple-card OF parser and
full ASoC card registration with two same-named CPU DAIs and a shared codec
DAI. It registers distinct playback PCM 0 and capture PCM 1 devices, while
a separate single-link card retains its original runtime name. This checks
card-registration and naming behavior without emulating the audio hardware.

Tested on a physical PW-SA3 with synchronized DMA termination support:

* Thirteen normal 44.1 kHz S16_LE captures at gain settings 0, 4 and 7
  returned the expected frame counts: 88,200 for two seconds and 132,300
  for three seconds. The microphone signal occupied the left slot and the
  right slot remained zero.
* Two paused-stream closes completed after 8,820 frames each, followed by
  successful capture. A second open of the same PCM was refused with
  ``-EBUSY``.
* One card-only reload and three full card/codec reloads completed with
  successful capture afterwards. Gain readback matched the selected value.
* Capture power registers returned to idle after close. Clock and supply
  consumer references returned to their starting values after teardown.
* Twenty observed DMA teardown snapshots had zero semaphore count and idle
  channel state. No kernel fault reports occurred during the tests.

These results cover the simplified capture codec and synchronized DMA-stop
path, before the additional normal-mode settling delay and speaker support.
They do not validate the updated codec, SAIF clock-provider changes or
production card/board integration. Real
supply switching and sequencing, live provider unbind, system PM and
clock-fault recovery remain unvalidated.

A subsequent user-reported check on a physical PW-SA3 with the Buildroot
``fix1`` image and kernel ``6.1.70-brain-audio-fix1`` confirmed that
``/audio.sh`` worked: five-second recording followed by speaker playback.
This provides basic end-to-end confirmation of the corrected generic-card
integration. No additional frame-count, audio-quality, clock-pin or power-rail
measurements were supplied with that confirmation; the exclusions above
still apply.
